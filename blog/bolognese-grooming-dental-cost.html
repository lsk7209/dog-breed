Short answer

Bottom line: a Bolognese fits a household ready to fund steady coat care, attend to dental health, and accept that a rare breed can complicate sourcing and pricing. Build a repeatable grooming routine, ask about dental care early, verify records where possible, and keep a reserve for costs that arrive before you expect them, so the first year is planned rather than improvised.

Decision boundary and next step

The narrow decision is whether your household can keep up the Bolognese coat and dental routine on an ordinary week and absorb the uncertainty a rare breed brings, with a named person for each task. If the first-year plan feels shaky, pause and act: get a grooming quote, or bring your dental and sourcing questions to a veterinarian before treating this breed as a fit.

What not to overclaim

Do not treat this guide as a diagnosis, a purchase recommendation, or a promise that one breed will be cheaper than another for every household. Local prices, individual dogs, breeder or rescue records, training history, and veterinary advice can change the final decision.
