What this hub measures

This hub groups three published U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics Consumer Price Index series: pet food and treats, purchases of pets and pet supplies or accessories, and pet services. Each data note identifies its series ID, latest reported period, index value, and change calculations returned with that series. The pages are built from the BLS Public Data API rather than store listings or a private price survey.

An index is useful for seeing direction and change across a defined national series. It is not a dollar quote, local price list, forecast, or statement about what one dog will cost. The BLS catalogue describes these series as U.S. city average and not seasonally adjusted. A household should not convert an index level into a monthly bill or assume that it represents a nearby clinic, groomer, boarding provider, trainer, retailer, shelter, or breeder.

How to read a data note

  1. Start with the category that matches the decision: food for recurring feeding, supplies for setup and replacement gear, or services for support such as grooming, boarding, walking, and care assistance.
  2. Check the reported period before using the number. The site shows the date recorded in the saved public-data snapshot; it is not a live quote.
  3. Use the 12-month change as a budget stress test, then write down the local price evidence that would actually determine the household plan.
  4. Keep separate lines for routine expenses, setup purchases, and an uncertainty reserve. A national CPI series cannot decide whether a particular service is necessary for a particular dog.

What to verify locally

Collect the local facts that the public index cannot supply: food format and amount, replacement timing for crates or safety gear, grooming or boarding policies, availability of backup care, and any written rule that affects the household. For health, diet, medication, or treatment questions, use the relevant qualified professional rather than inferring an answer from CPI movement.

Source and update record

The underlying series are retrieved through the BLS Public Data API v2. For definitions and release context, consult the BLS CPI program and API documentation. BreedWise records the snapshot update date above each hub and data-note page. If a source changes, a series becomes unavailable, or a page overstates what the series supports, send the URL and source detail through Corrections.

Reading categories together

Food, supplies, and services are separate series, not a breed score. Use them to ask whether the recurring food plan has room to move, whether setup assumes unrealistically cheap replacement gear, and whether a schedule depends on paid support. Compare like with like: the same series, reported period, and change window. Avoid comparing an index value from one category with a cash price from another.

Editorial boundary

BreedWise publishes educational planning material. This hub does not diagnose pets, recommend treatment, rank insurers, promise affordability, or decide whether a household should acquire a dog. Read the methodology for source, local-record, and professional-question boundaries.
